Offee

Your paperwork, done on machines you own.

Offee fills in forms, drafts replies, translates letters and sums up meetings. It is self-hosted: the AI doing it runs on your own computer if you work for yourself, or on a server you own if you work as a team. Your passport scan, payslip or contract never goes to someone else's server.

What you need, and how to install

A Mac with Apple Silicon and macOS 13 or later, or a Linux PC on x64 or arm64 with virtualization on. 16 GB of memory and 25 GB of free disk. The source code is public; this is an early test build.

On a Mac

  1. Open Offee-arm64.dmg and drag Offee to Applications.
  2. The preview is not notarized yet, so the first time right-click Offee and choose Open. On macOS 15, open it once, then allow it in System Settings, Privacy and Security.
  3. Choose Set up Offee. It downloads the AI (about 10 GB) once, then opens. From then on it needs no internet.

On Linux

  1. Make sure virtualization is on (/dev/kvm exists), then install Colima and the Docker command line tools with Compose.
  2. On Debian or Ubuntu, install the package with sudo apt install ./Offee-linux-x64.deb. ARM computers and other distributions take another package from the Linux preview release: Offee-linux-arm64.deb, or a .tar.gz to unpack as the README explains.
  3. Open Offee from your applications and choose Set up Offee. If your desktop shows no tray icon, open Offee again to bring its window back.

How it works

A locked office inside your computer: Offee works only on what you hand it, and has no line to the outside unless you open one.

Try the switch

Nothing leaves your computer. Every answer comes from the AI on your computer. This is how Offee starts, every time.

Each request waits for you. When a task could use an online AI, Offee shows exactly what would be sent. You say yes or no, one request at a time.

Requests go out for a few minutes, then it seals itself again. Each one is still written down, so you can see later what left.

Where your information lives

Why not just use a search engine or an online AI?

Question An online AI or search engine Offee
Where your documents go To the company's servers, under its rules, sometimes used to train its next model. Nowhere. They stay on your computer, or your team's server, unless you let one request out.
What it knows The web. Not your rental contract or last week's meeting. The files you gave it, and it shows which one each answer came from.
What you get Text to copy and paste into the form yourself. The form itself, filled in and, if you like, signed.
What it needs An account and an internet connection. No account. Once installed, no internet.
Checking its work You trust what you are shown. Every task is logged: what went in, which AI answered, whether anything left.

The honest trade: an AI small enough for a laptop is slower than the big online ones and can be wrong. That is why Offee always shows its suggestions for you to check first.

Getting started

Once Offee is set up, create a profile, or let Offee read one from a document, and try a form. Two made up documents to practise with are in the samples folder, and the app offers them with one click.

For a team, on a server you own

A small or medium team can run one Offee on a Linux server it controls, in the office or at home, and everyone opens it from their own laptop. It is not built to be put on the internet, and running it on a server does not change that:

  • A private network is the only way in. Offee on the server answers only the server itself. You reach it through Tailscale, a private network between your own devices, or through an SSH tunnel if you do not use Tailscale. No port is opened for Offee, and there is no login page, so nobody outside can get to it.
  • There are no user accounts. Everyone you let onto that private network, or who can log in to the server, sees every profile and document and can turn the switch. It suits a team that already shares its paperwork; give a group that must keep things apart a server of its own.
  • Your documents live on the server, protected by the server's disk encryption, not your own computer's.

What the server needs, and how to deploy

The server needs Linux with virtualization, Colima and Docker, 16 GB of memory and 25 GB of free disk, and you need SSH access to it. From a copy of the source code on your laptop:

./bin/offee remote me@server deploy
./bin/offee remote me@server tailscale on

The first command checks the server and stops before downloading anything if it falls short, then sets Offee up there and makes sure it does not answer on the server's own address. The second makes Offee available over HTTPS to the devices on your Tailscale network, and refuses while Tailscale Funnel, which would publish it to the internet, is on. Without Tailscale, ./bin/offee remote me@server tunnel opens it through SSH instead.

Tailscale's coordination service learns which of your devices exist and when they connect, but never sees what they send. The README has the details, including how to harden SSH and how to keep even that in house.
